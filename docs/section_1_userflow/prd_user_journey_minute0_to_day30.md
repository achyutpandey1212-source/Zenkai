# PRD — User Journey (Minute 0 to Day 30)

## Product Thesis

People do not fail because they lack ambition. They fail because they carry too much cognitive load alone.

Our product exists to reduce that load by becoming a trusted AI growth partner that:
- learns how the user works,
- protects long-term goals,
- reduces decision fatigue,
- and feels calm, consistent, and alive.

---

## 1) User Journey: Minute 0 to Day 30

### Minute 0 — Discovery / Landing Page
The user lands on the product for the first time.

**What they see**
- A clean, calm, premium landing page.
- One strong message, not a feature dump.
- Primary CTA: **Meet your Growth Partner**.

**What the product should communicate instantly**
- This is not another noisy productivity app.
- This AI will help the user carry less mental load.
- The experience is conversational, personal, and trustworthy.

**User emotion**
- Curious.
- Slightly relieved.
- Not overwhelmed.

---

### Minute 1 — Sign In
The user signs in with Google.

**Design goal**
- No friction.
- No long form.
- No unnecessary setup.

**Product rule**
- Authentication should feel invisible.
- The user should move from landing to value as fast as possible.

---

### Minute 2 — First Conversation Starts
Instead of showing forms, tasks, or dashboards, the app opens with a conversational welcome.

**The AI says**
- A warm greeting.
- A simple explanation of what it does.
- One core question: **What are you trying to become?**

**Why this matters**
- The product begins with identity, not tasks.
- It immediately feels more human than a standard planner.

**What the AI is trying to learn**
- The user’s current mission.
- Their long-term direction.
- Their main pressure points.
- Their preferred work rhythm.

---

### Minute 3 to 5 — Understanding the User
The user answers a few guided questions in chat.

**The AI should uncover**
- Main goals.
- Current deadlines.
- Recurring struggles.
- Work style.
- Peak focus time.
- Existing commitments.

**Important**
- This must feel like a conversation, not an interrogation.
- The product should ask only what it truly needs.
- The user should feel understood, not profiled.

**Examples**
- “What is the biggest thing you are working toward right now?”
- “What usually gets in your way?”
- “When do you usually work best?”
- “What deadlines already matter this week?”

---

### Minute 6 — First Proactive Plan Appears
The AI auto-generates the first weekly structure.

**What the user sees**
- A first draft of their plan.
- Not a blank workspace.
- Not a request to manually create everything.

**Why this is important**
- The product proves it does work before the user asks for it.
- This is the first “wow” moment.
- The user starts to feel the AI is proactive, not passive.

**User emotion**
- Surprise.
- Relief.
- Trust.

---

### Day 1 — Daily Briefing Experience
When the user returns the next day, the home page opens as a calm briefing.

**The home page should**
- greet the user by name,
- summarize the situation clearly,
- surface only the most important priorities,
- reduce everything else into background noise.

**Home page structure**
- Greeting.
- Today’s priorities.
- One or two risks.
- One clear next action.

**Example**
- “Good evening, Achyut.”
- “You have a lot going on, but I’ve narrowed it down to 3 things.”
- “Start with the assignment first. That will reduce pressure for the rest of the day.”

**Product goal**
- The AI absorbs the stress.
- The user gets clarity.

---

### Days 2 to 7 — Trust Formation Week
This is the stage where the product earns belief.

**What the AI should do**
- Notice patterns.
- Make increasingly relevant suggestions.
- Occasionally explain its reasoning.
- Adapt based on what worked and what did not.

**What the AI should learn**
- Which kinds of tasks the user delays.
- Which time of day the user is most effective.
- How much structure the user prefers.
- What usually causes overload.

**Tone**
- Calm.
- Confident.
- Never robotic.
- Never overly emotional.

**User emotion**
- “This is actually starting to get me.”

---

### End of Week 1 — Weekly Reflection
Instead of a corporate report, the product offers a reflective check-in.

**What it should include**
- A few wins.
- One challenge.
- One learning.
- One adjustment for next week.

**Goal**
- Reinforce progress.
- Make the user feel seen.
- Keep the product from feeling like a cold task tracker.

**Desired effect**
- The user sees growth, not just task completion.

---

### Week 2 — Better Predictions, Better Support
By now the product should have enough behavioral signals to become more opinionated.

**What changes**
- The AI begins to recommend with more confidence.
- It starts narrowing choices instead of presenting too many options.
- It protects the user from overcommitting.

**Example behavior**
- If the user tries to overload the week, the AI should say:
  - “This week is already crowded. I recommend moving this to Saturday.”
- If the user is underworking:
  - “You have room for one more focus block today.”

**Product goal**
- Reduce decision fatigue.
- Keep the user moving forward without mental clutter.

---

### Week 3 — Proactive Protection Mode
The AI starts acting more like a guardrail.

**What it should do**
- Detect overload early.
- Spot deadline clashes.
- Suggest trade-offs before problems get worse.
- Protect focus time automatically where possible.

**Example**
- The user adds a new hackathon or assignment.
- The AI evaluates the impact.
- It offers a safer schedule instead of just accepting everything blindly.

**Why this matters**
- The product starts to feel like it is working for the user, not just with them.

---

### Day 30 — The First Growth Reflection
After a month, the product delivers a meaningful reflection.

**What it should show**
- Progress over time.
- Consistency improvements.
- Fewer missed deadlines.
- Better focus habits.
- A stronger sense of control.

**Tone**
- Proud, but not fake.
- Supportive, but not cheesy.
- Honest about both wins and gaps.

**Example**
- “When we first met, you were carrying too much in your head.”
- “Now you are more consistent.”
- “You are still ambitious, but you are managing that ambition better.”
- “Let’s keep building.”

**Why this matters**
- The AI becomes part of the user’s growth story.
- The relationship deepens through continuity, not gimmicks.

---

## 2) Daily Habit That Brings Users Back
The product should encourage a short evening ritual.

### Evening Ritual
Every day, the app invites the user to spend 3 minutes sharing how the day went.

**The AI asks**
1. What are you most proud of today?
2. What got in your way today?
3. Is there anything I should remember for the future?

**Why this works**
- It is low-friction.
- It builds memory naturally.
- It helps the AI understand patterns over time.
- It makes the AI feel alive and consistent.

---

## 3) Core Principles for This Journey
- The AI should reduce cognitive load.
- The AI should deepen trust.
- The AI should feel calm, premium, and alive.
- The AI should be proactive, not passive.
- The AI should understand the user’s story over time.
- The experience should feel like a trusted partner, not a dashboard.

---

## 4) Non-Goals for This Phase
- No noisy dashboard.
- No feature overload.
- No complex onboarding forms.
- No “AI therapist” positioning.
- No gimmicky emotional manipulation.
- No unnecessary tabs or clutter.

---

## 5) Success Signal
If the user closes the app and thinks:

> “I don’t have to carry all of this in my head anymore, and I know what I should work on now.”

then the product is working.
Every meaningful conversation should make tomorrow's plan better.
